Thesis

One rule holds the whole design together

The app answers a single question, and colour is how it answers. So colour gets split into two jobs that never overlap.

Semantic colour means something. Green, red and amber are reserved — permanently — for sign and state. Positive balance, negative balance, marginal. They are never used to decorate a heading, brand a button, or fill a chart just because a chart needs fills.

Accent colour means interaction. A single mulberry carries focus rings, the active tab, links, the add-row, the primary button. It never carries meaning. If a number is mulberry, that is a bug.

Why it matters here: the brief asks the user to read the answer from colour before reading a digit. That only works if the answer is the only thing coloured. Every generic finance dashboard breaks this by colouring categories, and then green stops meaning "good".

The consequence for a warm palette specifically: the ground is plaster, the ink is warm, and the semantics have rotated to olive, brick and ochre — which uses up most of the warm end of the wheel. The accent has to sit clear of all three, so it goes to mulberry: the one warm-leaning hue with real distance from every meaning, and rich enough on putty to read as a considered choice rather than a leftover.

System

The token block is the deliverable

Every colour in the app resolves through a variable in one :root block, and Tailwind consumes the variables rather than hex values. Swapping the palette later is one block, not a sweep. No raw hex exists outside it.

Swapping the palette means editing nine lines. If the mulberry is wrong, try #8E3050 (wine — warmer, but check it against the brick red at 8 px) or #5B4BA8 (warm indigo — cooler, and the safest distance from every semantic). Do not move the accent into rust, ochre or olive: on this ground they are indistinguishable from red, amber and green, and the accent would start looking like a verdict.

Warming the ground moved three other things. Pure white left the palette — --raised is now #FFFDF8, because a true white input on plaster reads as a hole rather than a lift. The semantics all rotated toward earth: olive-forest, brick, ochre. And the ink went warm too (#1E1813), which matters more than it sounds — a neutral black header over a warm ground is the single thing that makes a warm palette look accidental.

Resumen · desktop · 3 statesSwitch above

The positive state is not the negative state with a bigger number. When the balance is positive, the sixth KPI stops being "meses de margen" — runway is meaningless without a deficit — and becomes colchón: the buffer target, whether it is covered, and what is left after moving in. Only when the balance goes negative does that slot become months of runway. The label changes with the state; the slot does not move.

The marginal state gets its own framing too. At +18 €/month the buffer is technically covered and the answer is still fragile, so the sub-line quantifies the fragility in the units the user actually feels: "un imprevisto de 200 € se come once meses de margen".

Frequency and monthly equivalent, without doubling the noise. Importe holds the number the user actually knows — "the water bill is 58 € every two months" — and Equiv. mensual holds the number they decide with. The divisor sits between them as a faint mono ÷2 / ÷12. That one annotation removes all ambiguity about which column was normalised, at almost no visual cost, and it is the same glyph toMonthly() is doing internally.

Price history without a modal. A row with more than one revision grows a small delta chip next to its amount (▲ 14 %). The caret expands a drawer row underneath — same table, no overlay, no lost context. Three lines of mono per revision: date, amount, and what changed against previous and original.

Blank amounts are the normal state, so they are styled, not hidden. An empty amount renders as a dashed — — in faint, its equivalent shows — — too, and the panel header counts coverage: 11 / 14 con importe. The Resumen KPI repeats that count under the total. A number computed from partial data is labelled as such rather than presented as final.

Pausado stays visible. The row dims to 50 % and its equivalent reads no cuenta instead of a number. Removing it from the table would lose the decision; greying it keeps "I could add the gym back" one click away.

Costes · card per row, third card tapped open

Two lines per card, and only two. Line one is what you look for and what it costs. Line two is category, real frequency and the monthly equivalent — the same ÷2 annotation as desktop. Everything else (dirección, prioridad, estado, nota, historial) appears only when the card is tapped open, in place, pushing the list down. No modal, no separate edit screen, no lost scroll position.

Status moves from a select to a 3 px left edge. On desktop a coloured select is scannable; on a phone it costs a whole column. The edge stripe reads at arm's length, and the select itself reappears inside the opened card where there is room for it. Same information, different carrier.

The totals row becomes a sticky footer card in --sunk, directly above the add-row. It never scrolls out of reach, because on a phone the whole reason for editing a number is to watch that one figure move.

Breakpoint is 820 px, and it is a real fork. Desktop keeps the dense table at 12 px; mobile renders a different component from the same Entry[]. Trying to make one table responsive into both is where this design would go wrong — the grid and the card list are two views of one calculation layer, and src/lib/ does not know which one is mounted.

Decisions

Where a domain rule became a visual rule

Each trap in docs/DESIGN-BRIEF.md §4 has a specific consequence in the layout above. Listing them together so a future change can be checked against them.

Fianza is refundable
Two figures, never one
Dinero al entrar and gasto real are separate KPIs and separate ledger totals, with the fianza row carrying a green Devolvible pill. The margin calculation subtracts upfrontCash from savings but never counts the fianza as burned. There is no combined "moving costs" number anywhere in the design, in any tab.
Runway only means something when the balance is negative
The sixth KPI changes identity with the sign
Negative → meses de margen. Positive → colchón, showing the target, whether it is covered, and what is left. Marginal → the same slot quantifies fragility instead ("un imprevisto de 200 € se come once meses"). "∞ meses" can never render, because that branch does not exist.
Max affordable rent is a rule of thumb
A guideline strip, not a validation error
It sits in amber below the KPI row, states its own assumption inline (32 % sobre 730 €/mes), and never touches an input or blocks a save. In the mockup all three scenarios exceed it and two of them still work — which is exactly the point, and the copy says so.
Agency fees are the landlord's by law
Present, at zero, flagged red
The row exists in the upfront ledger with 0,00 € struck through and a red No deberías pagarlo pill. Omitting it entirely would leave the user to be surprised by a listing that charges one; budgeting it silently would legitimise it. Showing it at zero teaches the rule.
Electricity is seasonal · promos expire · rent rises
The nota column is load-bearing, so it is wide and always visible
Not a tooltip, not an icon, not truncated to an ellipsis: a full editable column carrying "media anual · enero ~75 €" and "promo 12 m · jun-27 sube a 39 €". These are the notes that stop a plausible-looking budget being quietly wrong, and hiding them behind a hover defeats them on a phone entirely.
The empty state is the normal state
Coverage is displayed next to every total
Blank amounts render as a dashed — — rather than 0,00 € — a zero is a claim, a dash is an admission. Panel headers count 11 / 14 con importe; the Salidas KPI repeats it; Muebles gets a whole KPI for sin precio, because the "mínimo para entrar" figure is only as true as its coverage.
No rate or price is hardcoded
Every number in these mockups is user data
Taxes and local charges are ordinary rows with an editable amount and a nota. The only constant in the whole design is the 32 % guideline percentage, and that lives in Ajustes as an editable field with its assumption printed beside it.
Income is lumpy and nothing is fixed
The income side is designed to look uncertain
Entradas is not a single confident figure: the KPI sub-line reads "beca · familia · trabajo — ninguno es fijo", and the rows carry their own caveats ("hasta jun-27, sin renovación segura", "media de 6 meses · no es fijo"). A design that renders 730 € as solidly as a salary would be lying about the thing that matters most here.
Build order

What to write, in order

This is a real sequence: each step is only safe once the one above it exists. CLAUDE.md already names the first two — this expands the rest and maps each to the screens above.

  1. Scaffold and the token blockvite + src/styles/tokens.css + tailwind.config.tsThe nine lines of palette, the three font stacks, Tailwind wired to consume variables. Nothing else can be styled correctly before this, and a raw hex added now is a hex someone has to hunt down later.
  2. The modelsrc/types.tsEntry, Scenario, PurchaseProject, Buffer, and the enums behind every select in the mockups. Single source of truth (CLAUDE.md § Domain model).
  3. The calculation layersrc/lib/money.ts · frequency.ts · derive.tsInteger cents (IND001), toMonthly() (IND004), sign from direction (IND005), and every figure in the KPI row. Pure functions, no React. This is what Vitest covers and it can be finished before a single component exists.
  4. Storagesrc/lib/storage.tsThe only module touching localStorage (IND006). Schema version, DEFAULTS() and ensureShape() together (IND003), JSON export/import through the same door.
  5. The string tablesrc/i18n/es.tsEvery Spanish label visible in the mockups above, in one file (IND008). Writing it now means components are built against keys from the start instead of being swept later.
  6. Component sheetsrc/components/Panel, KpiCard, Tag, Button, EditableCell, StatusSelect, BreakdownBar, AddRow. Build them against the sheet above, not against a screen — every tab is assembled from these ten and reaches for nothing else.
  7. Costessrc/tabs/Costes.tsxBefore Resumen, deliberately: Resumen has nothing to summarise until data can be entered, and the editable grid is where the state model gets stress-tested.
  8. Resumensrc/tabs/Resumen.tsxAll three verdict states and the sign-dependent sixth KPI. Every figure read from src/lib/, never computed inline (IND007).
